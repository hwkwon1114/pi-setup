# Cross-domain idea transfer

Borrow a mechanism, not merely a metaphor. Use this for science, engineering, design or other problem-solving; do not force every inquiry into a research-paper format.

## Frame the transferable problem

Start from the user's intent and relevant existing evidence. Restate the bottleneck without disciplinary jargon: what must be inferred, controlled, represented, coordinated or created; which constraints and failure modes matter? Treat this abstraction as provisional. Keep domain-specific details that could invalidate a transfer.

## Find useful connections

Consider adjacent and distant fields with similar problem structure. Search by relationships and mechanisms as well as original terminology—for example, delayed feedback, redundant measurements or allocation under uncertainty. These are search lenses, not mandatory categories or evidence of applicability.

Use inspected sources and existing knowledge first. For one supplied paper, use `paper-summary` and `pdf-read` as appropriate. For substantive multi-paper discovery/review, follow the host's literature delegation route and `research-workflow` handoff guidance; supply the target bottleneck, candidate source-field terminology, constraints and existing evidence. Do not bypass role-only tools.

Consider informal practices, artifacts and designs when relevant; papers are not the only possible sources. Label unverified leads and independently generated ideas. Do not impose a minimum number of fields, papers or candidates, or reward disciplinary distance for its own sake.

## Translate and challenge

For each promising connection:

- **Mechanism:** What makes the source approach work? Identify the supporting passage, equation, artifact or example when inspected; otherwise label it a conjectural analogy.
- **Mapping:** Map the important entities, relationships and operations into the target setting. Explain what would actually change, rather than only renaming terms.
- **Assumptions and breaks:** Which conditions are necessary, available, missing or incompatible? Check scale, observability, incentives, information, resources and causality when consequential. Include a counterexample or failure condition.
- **Discriminating check:** What small derivation, existing-data check, prototype or experiment could distinguish this transfer from the current approach or a rival explanation? State the expected outcomes and what they would not establish.

Different problems need different checks; do not force numerical metrics onto a conceptual or design question. A source's successful result is not evidence that the target transfer works. Reject or narrow attractive but structurally weak analogies.

## Return the useful ideas, not the process

Lead with the strongest defensible connection and a few alternatives only if they offer genuinely different mechanisms. Explain why a connection helps, its decisive limitation and the next useful check. An unresolved assumption can make a promising idea conditional; lack of evidence is not proof it fails.

A compact transfer note, only when useful:
```text
Target bottleneck:
Borrowed mechanism / source and access status:
Target mapping / proposed change:
Assumptions / where the analogy breaks:
Smallest discriminating check / possible outcomes:
Status: sourced observation, inferred transfer or speculative proposal; not validated
```

Default to a concise chat answer; update existing notes only when requested or expected. Separate source claims, your inferences and proposed transfers. Do not claim novelty, causality or general performance from analogy alone. Propose tests without silently launching them; actual execution follows existing scope, resource limits and review requirements. This skill creates no new permissions or runtime enforcement.
