# Research control: relevance, authorization and restoration

Use before implementing a new investigation, before a new research stage or
expensive execution, and after compaction/resumption, branch changes or handoffs.
This is a procedure, not a launcher, objective author or experiment permission.
Apply it to research; ordinary maintenance needs no scientific protocol.

## Restore the authoritative state

Read the project's objective entry point and active investigation record, not
just a conversation summary. Reuse existing notes/protocols rather than adding
a parallel catalog. Restore the ultimate objective, current approved question,
task connection, non-goals, data/resource limits, decisions/rejected directions,
evidence and limits, unresolved conflicts, review status and next action.

A missing or ambiguous current question blocks new scientific execution, not
safe inspection or ordinary edits. Ask only for blocking decisions; continue
authorized work autonomously within its scope. Explicit researcher changes can
revise the objective; retain their provenance and do not rigidly enforce an old
objective against a new request.

## Gate A: scientific alignment before implementation

Record a short alignment statement in the existing investigation/protocol:

1. **Question and target:** Which scientific quantity, estimand and domain?
   What is the link to the authoritative objective?
2. **Rival explanations:** What does each possible outcome distinguish?
   What relevant decision changes if the result is positive, negative or unresolved?
3. **Role:** Direct scientific evidence, a necessary supporting diagnostic,
   or a separate question? Supporting work names its approved dependency,
   acceptance check and stopping rule. A separate question is proposed, not launched.
4. **Evidence and design:** Check saved same-version evidence and its limits
   first. Declare fixed/varied factors, information access, independent units
   and the smallest distinguishing test; do not silently substitute a proxy.
5. **Authority and bounds:** Identify approved scope, cumulative budget,
   stopping rules, exclusions, sealed-test policy and next permitted action.

Non-identifiability and negative outcomes may answer the scientific question.
Good predictions, narrow intervals or passing numerical checks cannot by
themselves prove physical attribution. Resolve consequential recipe conflicts;
do not optimize an irrelevant experiment more carefully.

## Gate B: execution readiness

Before the host's review-triggering execution, supply the reviewer the objective,
active record, approved protocol, code/config/data/design identities, tests,
information limits and resource/selection/resumption policies. Follow the
host's reviewer routing and numerical/device contract, not a universal model,
precision or architecture prescribed by this skill.

Wait for the completed verdict. Resolve launch blockers, record caveats and
actual checks, and bind approval to reviewed scope/identities. Missing, failed
or stale review is not approval. Consequential code, configuration, data,
objective, recipe or scope changes require reassessment of affected conditions.
An approval cannot expand budget, create experimental permission or release
tests outside the declared policy. Use the training/checkpoint checklist when
applicable; it comes after relevance, not instead of it.

Small, explicitly bounded correctness fixtures and quick edits follow the
host's lightweight-check exception. Declare their ceiling before execution;
repetition must not become an unreviewed training campaign.

## Handoffs and completion

Pass delegates the objective/state paths and versions, bounded task, relevance,
non-goals, edit boundary, information/resource limits, known rejected directions,
expected evidence/decision and stop/ask conditions. Child results are evidence
or proposed deltas; the coordinator owns integration and authorization checks.

At completion, preserve artifacts, failures and negative results, review claims
under the project checklist, and update the current entry point with actual
status and next authorized action. Do not strengthen claims from execution
success alone or duplicate historical evidence into new authoritative sources.

## Behavioral regression cases

Exercise at least: prediction improves without parameter recovery; unrelated
architecture sweep; necessary bounded gradient diagnostic; stale/misleading
summary; changed identities after approval; missing/quota-blocked reviewer;
explicit researcher objective change. Include a safe ordinary-edit case.

Distinguish source/format tests, model decision tests with synthetic summaries,
actual native compaction/resume tests and launch-enforcement tests. Passing one
does not establish the others, natural skill selection or universal drift
prevention. Keep case prompts, expected outcomes and raw responses separately
so a responder is not simply copying an answer key.
