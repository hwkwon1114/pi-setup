# Project kickoff: purpose before experiments

Use when starting a research project, establishing its first investigation, or
repairing a missing purpose anchor. Apply across empirical research, benchmarks,
method development, theory and exploratory inquiry. This is guidance, not an
automatic hook, experiment launcher or guarantee against drift.

## 1. Reuse or establish the anchor

Read the researcher's request, project instructions and existing notes first.
Identify one stable objective entry point and one current investigation record;
these can be sections of the same document. Preserve existing names, history and
researcher notes. Do not generate a document suite or migrate an established
project just to fit a template.

If an anchor is missing, draft a short purpose statement from explicit researcher
intent and label inferred details as provisional. Ask only about consequential
ambiguities before committing to experimental implementation; thinking, source
inspection and exploratory discussion need not wait for a finalized protocol.
Record the source/date of the direction. Never invent an approved objective.

Use the optional [objective template](../templates/OBJECTIVE.template.md) and
[investigation template](../templates/INVESTIGATION.template.md) as sections or
standalone notes. Template filenames are examples, not required project paths.

## 2. Define what would count as progress

Capture the long-term question, why its answer matters, applicable domain and
assumptions, and the scientific decision the next evidence could change.
Distinguish the target claim or quantity from convenient measurements and
optimization diagnostics. An estimand is a defined quantity of interest, not
necessarily a metric; theory may need a proof or counterexample instead.

Choose evidence appropriate to the purpose. Prediction error can itself be the
scientific target in a forecasting study. When a metric is only a proxy, state
what connects it to the target, what could break that connection, and what remains
unmeasured. Paired metrics are useful when meaningful, not a universal requirement.
Do not force physical identification, a null hypothesis or numerical ground truth
onto every project.

State non-goals and likely drift traps: optimizing an easier metric, tuning a
favored method indefinitely, expanding infrastructure, or letting a supporting
diagnostic replace the inquiry. Candidate methods are means, not the purpose.

## 3. Connect the first investigation to a decision

Use [Gate A](research-control.md#gate-a-scientific-alignment-before-implementation)
in the current record. Choose the smallest useful distinguishing test or argument.
Name competing explanations, information access, relevant comparisons or controls,
uncertainty and evidence limits; a classical baseline is not compulsory for theory
or for every diagnostic.

For supportive, contrary/null and inconclusive outcomes, record what each would
support, leave unresolved, and change next. A negative result does not automatically
falsify a hypothesis: implementation failure, inadequate optimization, weak
measurement or insufficient precision may leave the question open. Do not diagnose
a mechanism from a proxy improvement alone or promise that any failure is publishable.

Label the work as direct evidence, a necessary supporting diagnostic, or a separate
question. A diagnostic names its dependency and ends when its check resolves.
Specify scientific stopping conditions and next action. No fixed compute budget is
required by this guide; honor actual host safety limits, allocations and information
restrictions. Execution and review permissions remain with the host.

## 4. Keep alignment alive without repeated paperwork

At session start, after resumption/compression, before a new investigation or
expensive execution, restore the objective and current record. At meaningful
milestones, ask: does the next action still address the question, or has the
method, metric or supporting task become the goal? Record only consequential
changes and unresolved issues. Do not reread the entire archive after every tool.

Before promoting conclusions, compare the evidence with the original question,
assumptions and outcome map. Check alternative explanations, matched information,
dependence, uncertainty and domain limits where applicable. Preserve negative and
inconclusive results. A successful run is not itself scientific success.

An explicit researcher-directed pivot is legitimate: update the anchor with its
rationale and history. Never rewrite the purpose retrospectively to match a result.
Follow [research control](research-control.md) for review, restoration and handoffs,
and [investigations and evidence](investigations-and-evidence.md) for records.
